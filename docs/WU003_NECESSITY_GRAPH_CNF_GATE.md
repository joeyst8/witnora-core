# WU-003 — Semantic necessity is not a first-UIP implication graph

**Status:** H3 falsified on ordinary CNF; restricted reason-subspace lemma
passes 147 exact traces. **No claim of novel proof complexity, SAT
acceleration, or resolved parity 1-UIP.**

## Source and testable question

Paul Beame and Glenn Sun, *Extending CDCL to Disjunctions of Parity
Equations*, SAT 2026, DOI `10.4230/LIPIcs.SAT.2026.5`, §3.2 and §8.
Algorithm 3 produces an **asserting** clause; the authors leave open
the precise analogue of graph-theoretic first-UIP and semantic clause
minimization for disjunctions of parity equations.

WU-001 ruled out using rank signatures alone. WU-002 showed that the
individual-coordinate support graph is not invariant under equivalent
parity representatives. WU-003 tests a third, basis-independent proposal.

## Rejected hypothesis H3

> Build a *semantic necessity graph*: an edge from earlier inference event
> `j` to later event `i` exists precisely when removing event `j`'s reason
> makes event `i` cease to propagate under exact unit closure. For a
> conflict event, the edge exists when removing the reason prevents that
> conflict. These edges suffice to recover classical graph first-UIP.

The closure and rank-one propagation are computed using the 2026 paper's
affine semantics; no Boolean assignments are enumerated. Because it works
with semantic unit closure and clauses rather than chosen row coordinates,
this proposed graph avoids WU-002's *syntactic* gauge problem. But it
loses the distinction between **direct antecedents** and **ancestors**.

## Exact counterexample (already within ordinary CNF)

Both traces start by deciding `a=1`, then propagate `b=1` and `c=1`, and
finally conflict on the **same clause** `¬b ∨ ¬c`.

| Reason for `c=1` | Direct implication-graph parents | True classical 1-UIP |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A: `¬b ∨ c` | `b` | `b` |
| B: `¬a ∨ ¬b ∨ c` | `a,b` | `a` |

Both use `¬a ∨ b` as the reason for `b=1`.

The exact semantic leave-one-out necessity graph is **identical** in the
two cases:

    a→b, a→c, b→c, a→conflict, b→conflict, c→conflict.

Why does it lose information? In A, the earlier decision `a=1` is
*indirectly necessary* for `c=1`: without `a`, the intermediate deduction
`b=1` disappears. In B, `a=1` is also an *explicit* antecedent of the
reason for `c`. Deleting `a` breaks the later inference in both traces,
so the necessity graph cannot distinguish these causally different cases.
Yet their genuine classical implication graphs have different 1-UIPs.

**Consequent no-go:** No algorithm whose *only* input is this necessity
graph can correctly reconstruct first-UIP on all classical traces.
Transitive reduction cannot repair lost information: the graph inputs
are already identical.

### Exact finite search

We enumerated a declared family of 147 four-variable selected-reason
traces. Each has one positive decision, three subsequent positive units
whose ordinary CNF reasons use nonempty subsets of preceding variables,
and a conflict clause falsified by the trail that includes the newest
variable. For each trace we computed exact XNF-style unit closure under
every single-event removal and compared the necessity signature to the
actual classical implication-graph first-UIP.

Results:

| Quantity | Result |
| --- | ---: |
| Finite CNF traces checked | 147 |
| Necessity signatures compatible with multiple actual 1-UIPs | 5 |
| Pairs of traces with identical necessity signature but differing actual 1-UIPs | 411 |

These are exact finite counts for this **restricted family**, not a
representative benchmark of SAT instances or a theorem about arbitrary
XNF traces.

## The surviving reason-intersection object

For each selected propagation, let `F_i` be the affine falsity span of its
**actual reason clause** and `P_i` the span of previous true units. Keep
the intrinsic subspace

    K_i = F_i ∩ P_i.

This retains information erased by mere leave-one-out necessity:

- A: `K_c = span(b=1)`, dimension one.
- B: `K_c = span(a=1, b=1)`, dimension two.

An elementary restricted lemma follows: if a CNF reason is the clause
`¬p1 ∨ ... ∨ ¬pk ∨ x` under a consistent trail with `p1,...,pk` true
and `x` unassigned, its falsity-space intersection with the prior true
unit span equals `span(p1,...,pk)` (where these are the satisfied affine
unit equations). Indeed the falsity span is generated by those `k`
premises and `x=0`, the latter independent of the prior span. The
intersection is therefore exactly the `k`-premise span. It is invariant
under changing the displayed affine generating equations.

The Rust suite verified the corresponding dimension/membership identities
across all 147 generated CNF traces. **This is elementary linear algebra,
not a new theorem, and it is not sufficient to prove parity 1-UIP.**

## Next frontier hypothesis H4 (not yet verified)

> Represent each inference as a **reason-local affine subspace hyperedge**:
> the *whole* premise space `K_i`, together with the one-dimensional
> quotient `F_i/K_i`, points to the newly established true-unit quotient.
> Seek a semantic *subspace cut* rather than a graph dominator of
> individual equations or an influence edge based on deleting reasons.

Three independent falsification obligations are now non-negotiable:

1. **CNF conservativity:** actual direct premise spaces and classical 1-UIP
   should be recovered, including cases with transitive dependencies.
2. **Gauge invariance:** the inferred cut must survive every equivalent
   row representative in WU-002 (and non-CNF cases beyond it).
3. **Proof soundness and uniqueness:** an alleged cut must yield an asserting
   Res(⊕)-checkable learned clause; exhibit a counterexample if cuts are
   nonunique or incomparable.

Only after these mathematical gates may we test proof size, clause
minimization cost, and runtime against published Xorcle. If any gate fails,
the hypothesis must be narrowed or rejected explicitly.
